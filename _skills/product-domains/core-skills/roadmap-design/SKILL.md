# Roadmap Design

## Purpose

Turn strategy and capabilities into an execution sequence. A good roadmap explains what is delivered, why it comes in that order, which dependencies matter, and how risk is reduced.

## Workflow

1. Start from objectives, product strategy horizons, customer journeys, and product-brick dependencies.
2. Group work into initiatives, releases, milestones, or roadmap items according to existing schema.
3. Sequence by customer value, business impact, technical dependency, operational readiness, and evidence needs.
4. Make MVP, expansion, migration, and KTLO paths explicit.
5. Link roadmap items to product bricks, teams, customers, KPIs, and discoveries where supported.

## Quality Bar

- The roadmap has a defensible order.
- Each item has an outcome or risk-reduction reason.
- Dependencies are concrete enough for teams to coordinate.
- Foundation work is justified by visible product or operating outcomes.
- Release plans are plausible for team capacity and domain maturity.

## Repository Fit

- Target files include `delivery/releases.json`, `product-bricks/roadmap/roadmap.json`, and objective initiative files.
- Preserve current schema variations by inspecting examples first.

## Avoid

- A backlog dump with no sequencing logic.
- Roadmap items that cannot be traced to goals, customers, capabilities, or bricks.
- Omitting migration, reliability, compliance, or data readiness when required for launch.
