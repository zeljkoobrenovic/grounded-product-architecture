## Boundary and value exchange

**Core:** processing inventories and reusable data dictionaries; business intake; DPIA, LIA and TIA records; human review and mitigation; data subject request and breach casework; supplier assessments and contract review; security assets, policies, controls and evidence; AI use-case inventories and screening; reporting, task coordination, access governance and tenant operations.

**Adjacent:** migration, training and adviser-assisted delivery; retention knowledge supplied by a specialist; consent tooling supplied through partners; customer identity providers, asset inventories, ticketing systems and regulatory submission channels. The public partner page identifies filerskeepers and Cookiebot. A named partnership does not establish the protocol, commercial entitlement or production scope of an integration. [Partners](https://privacyperfect.com/partners/)

**Excluded:** operating a customer's security operations center; executing deletion in every customer system; replacing the enterprise data estate; building or serving customer AI models; providing automatic legal approvals; certifying customers; regulator enforcement; a native consent-banner engine. Evidence imports and case tracking support these external responsibilities without claiming to perform them.

Organizations pay for governed workspaces and modules, with implementation and specialist support around the subscription. The public pricing page lists free and paid packages, user/record allowances, add-ons and an enterprise quotation route; prices and packaging are dated observations, not proposed prices or a claim that every current solution is included. [Packages and pricing](https://privacyperfect.com/pricing-page-for-all-packages/)

Privacy officers and security/AI owners gain a defensible decision record. Contributors provide accurate context without learning every legal framework. Sponsors gain visibility into unresolved exposure and adoption value. Advisers gain a repeatable client-delivery workflow. Individuals gain accessible ways to exercise rights; they neither buy a subscription nor need an account to submit a request. Regulators and independent auditors are external assurance recipients, not paying personas.

## Customer model

Four groups contain nine distinct personas:

- Accountability and assurance: privacy officer, security manager, AI governance owner and executive sponsor.
- Distributed contribution and administration: business process owner, supplier risk manager and tenant administrator.
- Expert delivery partners: external privacy adviser, with client-scoped access as a proposed operating boundary.
- People exercising rights: individual requester, including an employee or customer of the subscribing organization.

Each persona has operational jobs, a separate six-stage adoption journey, customer and provider-business KPI pyramids, and distinct year 1/3/5 strategies. Public sources support the roles and workflows; persona pains and purchase criteria are hypotheses for interviews. Business outcomes for non-buyers measure their contribution to the sponsoring account's sustainable service, not revenue charged to those people.

## Strategic thesis and sequencing

**Vision:** make it economical for organizations to keep a trustworthy, reviewable account of how they use personal data, suppliers and AI, and to act on that account when decisions or incidents arise.

The initial focus is European organizations whose small specialist teams depend on many occasional contributors. Compete through coherent records, approachable intake, controlled reuse, transparent scope and service-assisted adoption. Integration breadth and enterprise GRC breadth are tradeoffs, not assumed advantages over every competitor.

## Architecture and delivery assumptions

The architecture is a set of logical, ownable capabilities. Bricks need not become separate microservices, cloud accounts or databases. Shared services handle organization scope, identity, tasks, audit, evidence, integrations, reporting and retention. Domain services own processing, assessment, case, control, supplier and AI records. Stateless screening proposes review routes; an accountable human makes the decision.

The deployment model maps four observed solution areas and one modeled expert-delivery bundle to a browser experience and a proposed Netherlands-hosted runtime. It describes boundaries rather than naming an unverified cloud provider. Public forms expose only the intended intake or approved register projection. Administrative and adviser sessions remain tenant-scoped. Only approved, minimized data crosses connector boundaries; customer systems remain authoritative for actual operational data and remediation.

An initial delivery slice should establish isolation, import reconciliation, processing ownership, review scheduling and case evidence before extending to broad framework coverage. Integration outages must leave a visible last-success time, retry state and manual fallback. Notification failures must not silently mark a legal response as delivered.

## Operating model assumptions

Eight capability-owning teams represent a mature planning scenario, with lightweight group leadership and differentiated roles. This is **not PrivacyPerfect's actual staffing**, nor a minimum launch requirement. The organization can initially combine these responsibilities in fewer teams and split them only when workload and specialist capacity justify it. Customer legal decisions and independent audits stay outside the software delivery organization. Billing integrations support subscription administration; corporate finance, HR and sales are outside this domain's staffing total.

Every brick has one primary team in `teams.json`. Every data asset has a system-of-record brick, an owner and a steward. Human accountability covers template releases, privileged support, breach decisions, rights fulfillment and AI approval. No autonomous operational decision agents are assumed.

## Regulatory interpretation

Rights handling must preserve request scope, proportional identity checks, decision reasons and evidence of response. Breach handling distinguishes incident intake, awareness, risk evaluation and notification decisions; controller and processor duties differ. Local policies must be reviewed against authoritative rules. [EDPB rights guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/respect-individuals-rights_en), [EDPB breach guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/assess-the-risks/data-breaches_en)

AI governance must distinguish statutory obligations from voluntary risk frameworks, record the relevant version and context, and support review when use or rules change. This model does not hardcode AI Act application dates. [European Commission AI Act overview](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai), [NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework)
