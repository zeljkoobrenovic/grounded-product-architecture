Modeled on PastBook (Amsterdam, founded 2012 in the Rockstart Accelerator by Stefano Cutello; Vortex Capital Partners growth investment since December 2015). Sources: pastbook.com, help.pastbook.com, pastbook.com/newsroom, vortexcp.com/investment/pastbook, App Store / Google Play listings, Trustpilot, Business Wire and Tech.eu funding and award releases. Numbers below are sourced; anything not sourced is marked as a modeled assumption.

## What the business is

PastBook is a global "1-click photo book" platform. Consumers do not design a book; the platform does it for them. The app or website imports photos from the phone camera roll (by date, location, album or "Yearbook mode"), from Facebook (albums, cover and profile photos, timeline pictures and mobile uploads, one book per year from 2009 to 2025) or from a computer, Dropbox or Google Drive; the intelligent curation engine analyses image quality and context, removes duplicates and blurry shots, smart-groups photos into moments and lays out the pages in under 60 seconds; the customer previews the book for free, optionally edits, personalises the cover, theme colours and captions, shares a highlight video, and checks out. Print-ready files are routed to partner printing facilities on four continents that produce most orders in the country of the customer and ship to 150+ countries. Instagram import used to be a core channel and was withdrawn when Instagram discontinued third-party access for personal accounts, which is why the phone app and Facebook are now the primary sources.

Mission (newsroom): "make reliving memories frictionless". Tagline (home page): "Your Memories, Turned into Beautiful Photo Books in Seconds".

## Who the customers are

1. Consumers who keep memories: the family memory keeper who turns each year of phone photos into a yearbook, the traveller or milestone celebrant who wants a book right after a trip, wedding or graduation, the social-media archivist who prints a Facebook year in one click, and the seasonal gift giver who orders Year in Review books, calendars and posters against a holiday deadline.
2. PastBook's own operators: 24/7 support agents (WhatsApp, e-mail, chat), growth marketers running campaigns in ten languages with steep Q4 peaks, and the fulfilment and production operations managers who run the global print partner network.
3. Partners: partner printing facilities on four continents that receive print-ready orders, and the platforms and channels PastBook depends on (Meta for Facebook login and photos, Apple App Store and Google Play, Dropbox and Google Drive, payment providers, carriers).

## Value exchange

Consumers pay per printed product (no subscription) for the promise that a book exists in under a minute without design effort and arrives within days with premium print quality. PastBook keeps the gross margin between the retail price and the partner print and shipping cost, so unit economics depend on curation quality (fewer reprints and complaints), conversion from free preview to paid order, average pages and format upsell, promo discipline, print routing cost and customer acquisition cost. Print partners get steady, print-ready volume without owning the consumer relationship. Platforms provide the photo sources and distribution and set the policies PastBook must live within, as the Instagram withdrawal showed.

## Strategic tensions

- Dependence on platform APIs (Meta) and app-store rules versus owning the customer relationship through the app and account.
- Performance-marketing-led growth with 80% of customers in the US versus euro-based costs and a Q4-heavy seasonality that stresses print partners and support.
- Automated curation as the differentiator versus on-device "memories" features from Apple and Google and AI tools at large competitors (Mixbook Studio, Popsa).
- Low entry price and constant discounts versus print quality, delivery reliability and sustainability claims.

## Horizons (modeled)

- Year 1: raise preview-to-order conversion and repeat yearbook orders in the app, restore source breadth after the Instagram loss (phone, Facebook, cloud drives), stabilise peak-season production SLAs across partners.
- Year 3: an annual "memory habit" (yearbook reminders, calendars, gifting) with a high repeat rate, curation quality that removes the need to edit for most customers, and a partner network with redundant capacity per region.
- Year 5: the default way consumers turn any photo stream into a printed product in 150+ countries, with curation, personalisation and sustainability as the brand promise and print partners orchestrated as a utility.
