# Enable U Domain Review

Updated: 2026-09-05

## Product Domain Review

### Overall verdict

The model is ready for generation. It has a clear strategic spine: secure, managed interoperability lets public-sector and regulated customers modernize without losing continuity, trust or data ownership. Seven customer roles each carry two operational JTBDs, an evidence-shaped journey, measurable customer and business KPI pyramids, and explicit year-one, year-three and year-five horizons. Nine market-facing products map back to those roles. Public facts and analytical assumptions are separated in the domain brief, KPI descriptions, team headcount and competitive notes.

### Strongest areas

- Customer jobs describe observable work and end states rather than generic desires.
- The model distinguishes buyers, architects, builders, operators, data owners, transaction owners and ISV partners.
- Strategy connects customer outcomes to recurring managed services, reuse, platform attach and lifecycle economics.
- Insights cite primary Enable U and Vortex sources and link to the exact customers, jobs and KPI nodes they affect.
- Competition separates direct Dutch specialists from global platforms and recognizes Azure and Boomi as both partners and self-build substitutes.

### Evidence and assumptions

Company scale, portfolio, public-sector footprint, acquisitions and product characteristics are source-backed. KPI current values and targets, team structure, headcount allocation, internal architecture, margins, attach rates and roadmap horizons are explicitly modeled seeds. No invented revenue, EBITDA, pricing or market-share numbers are presented as fact. Competitive statistics are included only where an official company page provides them.

### Remaining research opportunities

- Validate customer-specific buying criteria and procurement cycles through interviews with municipalities, education institutions and regulated enterprises.
- Replace KPI seeds with anonymized service, delivery, adoption and commercial distributions.
- Test whether data management and trusted transactions are usually bought by existing integration customers or through separate buying centers.
- Add win/loss evidence against Yenlo, eMagiz, WeAreFrank!, global suites and customer self-build programs.

## Product Bricks Review

### Overall verdict

The implementation model is coherent and traceable. Ten outcome streams depend on twenty-nine product bricks grouped into integration delivery, API and trust, data and transactions, and managed operations. Every brick has supported layers and modules, explicit module-level dependencies, governed data dependencies, source evidence and a deployment role. Runtime-specific bricks remain bounded while catalog, contracts, observability, tenant control, trust, release and evidence provide the shared platform.

### Ownership and traceability

- Every brick has exactly one owning team and may be consumed by other teams as a service.
- Every product deployment names concrete deployed bricks and valid product references.
- Every customer JTBD step references a valid outcome stream; every flow step names the supporting bricks.
- Every governed data asset has a system-of-record brick, owner team, stewards, store, interfaces and governance policy.
- External dependencies are explicit at brick and stream level, including national facilities, PKIoverheid, Peppol, SNG, Azure, Boomi, Layer7 and customer endpoints.

### Architecture quality

The strongest decision is separating integration meaning—contract, mapping, policy, owner and outcome state—from runtime adapters. This supports the Vortex buy-and-build thesis and the public product mix without pretending all technologies should collapse into one engine. Residuality adds concrete primitives for sovereignty, certificate cohorts, government-profile versions, external backlogs, assurance evidence and acquired-product federation.

### Remaining engineering discovery

- Confirm the actual boundary between OpenTunnel, JNET capabilities, Azure accelerators and the managed integration control plane.
- Validate whether GGM, data distribution, Peppol and e-VOI share tenant, identity, monitoring and release services today.
- Replace conceptual stores and interfaces with evidence from repositories, cloud inventories and operating procedures when available.
- Add bricks-evidence and streams-evidence links to implementation repositories once internal evidence sources are connected.

## Teams Review

### Overall verdict

The modeled topology is organizationally plausible for the product, implementation, operations and enablement subset of a 180+ specialist company. Four groups and twelve teams have bounded missions, realistic 8–13 person team sizes, explicit customers and streams, one-owner brick coverage and visible dependencies. The topology avoids assigning public-sector protocols, identity policy, authoritative registers or Boomi internals to generic feature teams.

### Coverage and dependency quality

- Government Connectivity, API Trust and Identity, Boomi Platform, and Data Distribution and Registers isolate scarce complicated-subsystem knowledge.
- Managed Integration Platform and Platform Release and Security provide services other teams can consume instead of duplicating catalog, runtime patterns or assurance.
- Stream-aligned teams own Azure migration, business transactions, governed data products and service reliability outcomes.
- Consultancy and Customer Success is enabling: it turns assessment into adoption but does not own customer policy, legal decisions or production recovery.
- Data assets have owners and cross-team stewards aligned with operational responsibility.

### Human and AI boundaries

Automation may propose mappings, tests, anomaly cohorts, certificate actions and roadmap evidence. Humans remain accountable for business semantics, lawful purpose, access policy, security exceptions, irreversible replay, payroll or finance decisions, external incident communication and investment approval. These boundaries are recorded in the operating model and strategy horizons rather than inventing autonomous AI teams.

### Remaining organization discovery

- Verify actual leadership spans, locations, on-call rotations and how acquired company teams map to the portfolio.
- Measure cognitive load and dependency wait time between platform, implementation and service teams.
- Validate whether connector product management sits with runtime teams, sector teams or a central catalog owner.
- Rebalance modeled headcount once product revenue, incident load, delivery demand and platform ownership evidence is available.
